Proposal 2 · visual spec · iterating

Rebar — the HUD that isn't there

At rest the screen is the game. Nothing is pinned, nothing is permanent — every element is summoned by cursor, modifier, state or a peek key, and returns to zero alpha when it stops being true. One left-edge rail carries anything that happened while you weren't looking, and holds it, quietly, until you care.

The one rule
Geometry stays flat: 1px hairlines, no radius, no gradient, no fill above 14% except a data bar. All the life comes from alpha, plus a scale pulse and a slide on entrance. One texture in the whole design — a 32 × 32 soft bloom under the rail dots.
Recall without persistence
Hidden must never mean lost, so position is fixed forever and four independent channels bring things back: cursor proximity, alt held, state change, and a 3-second peek key. Muscle memory survives because nothing ever moves.
Combat is different, not more
Combat doesn't restore the resting HUD — it swaps in three facts and a rhythm: my health, my resource, my cast, my target's health, and a tick per tracked cooldown. No bars, no icons, no numbers unless something is wrong.
Scope, per your note
Procs are not exposed to addons, so nothing here claims to show one. Damage meters and ability suggestions stay Blizzard's. Rebar reads target, cast, cooldown and the notification events — that's it.
ROUND 4

The 12.1 rewrite — forwarding is legal, inspecting is not

Secret Values doesn't remove state from the design, it removes branching on state. A protected number can be handed to a widget setter, mapped through a curve, or used as a mask — it just can't be compared, sorted or measured in Lua. That kills exactly one thing in round 3 (priority encoded as height) and makes two others better than they were, because the escalation stops being three coded steps and becomes continuous. Below: the four mechanisms this rests on, then the low-state question as 4a / 4b, then the corrected rhythm line as 4c.

ColorCurveObject
Maps a secret value to a colour inside the protected bubble. This is the whole low-health treatment in one object: white → amber → red as a continuous ramp, no thresholds, no reads. Buys: graded colour. Can't: change size, alpha timing or geometry — those aren't colour.
MaskTexture, invertible
The bar's own fill texture becomes the mask for my texture — and inverted, it masks to the depleted region instead. Buys: anything drawn into either side of the bar's moving boundary. The void wash in 4a is this, and it costs no logic at all.
RegisterStateDriver
Discrete states arriving as attribute changes. The only mechanism that can fire a genuine event — which is why it's the only one that can drive a size change, a pulse, or the screen hairline. Costs: it's a step, so it pops.
CDM as hidden source
Cooldown Manager stays alive at zero alpha doing the tracking — procs, charges, recharge — and my own cells mirror it with forwarded values. Absolute layout freedom, no fight with the layout engine. Note: HasSecretValues() is a crash guard, not a signal.
4aContinuous — the bar is always telling you, quietly

One curve, no steps. A single ColorCurveObject on the health fill: neutral white at full, amber through the middle, danger red at the bottom. There is no 40%, no 20%, no 10% — the colour is a function, so it never pops and there's no boundary to sit on top of and flicker.
The void fills in behind. An inverted MaskTexture puts a red wash in the depleted region only — 0 to 0.22 alpha as the bar empties. The trough stops being empty space and starts being the thing that's eating you. Zero logic: the mask is the condition.
No size change, no pulse. Both need an event, and a curve isn't an event. The 12pt → 14pt tell from 3a is gone here, and so is the breathing. This is the honest cost of continuous.
Risk. It's ambient, and ambient things get tuned out — which is fine for a UI premised on staying invisible, and bad the one time you needed to be interrupted. Threshold ticks stay in as static geometry, so there's still a hard reference to read against.
4bDiscrete — silent, then loud. Same five moments, aligned with 4a above

Nothing, then everything. The first three frames are byte-identical to the resting HUD — the bar is white at 58% and at 34%, because a state driver has no opinion until it fires. One boundary, one moment, unmissable.
Events buy back the tells. Because this is a discrete state change, everything 4a had to give up comes back: 12pt → 14pt on the number, the 1.60s breathe on the health group, and the screen-edge hairline at 0.35 rising to 0.80 in the deepest state.
How many steps? Drawn here as one driver state, not three. Each additional step is another attribute and another visual vocabulary to justify — and the three-step version in 3a was already the part of round 3 I trusted least.
Risk. You get no warning approaching the line, and the step lands hardest exactly when you have least attention. Static ticks help, but a discrete design genuinely tells you less for the 75% of the fight that isn't an emergency.

They're combinable, and that may be the answer. The curve and the driver use different mechanisms and different channels — colour versus size, alpha and geometry. Running the ColorCurve ramp underneath a single driver state at the bottom gives ambient awareness the whole fight and one hard interrupt when it matters, without either one duplicating the other. It costs one more mechanism than 4a and one more visual than 4b. Say the word and I'll draw it as 4d.

Six cells, 38 × 4, gutter 2, uniform forever. Position equals ability because position is the only identity channel left — which was already true in 3e, so nothing about the read changes. What changes is that the cells are now my frames, not Blizzard's, so they can sit in a 240-unit line 18 units under the resource bar instead of wherever CDM wanted them.
HOW IT'S WIRED
CDM lives at alpha 0 and keeps doing its job — tracking, charges, recharge, procs. Each of my six cells is a Cooldown frame that receives forwarded values and never inspects one.

Total Lua arithmetic on combat state: none.

Token list gains cell 38 × 4, gutter 2 and edge cap 1px / 180ms; loses the 3 × 12 tick and every height variant. The colour table is unchanged — the rhythm line is still two alphas of one white.

Low health · edit mode for frames you can't see · the rail at eight · rail on the right · and the rhythm line. Each one is a proposal, not a decision — 3e in particular replaces something that already works.

Three-step escalation. Under 40%: the number appears in danger red and the health group breathes 1.0 ↔ 0.55 at 1.60s. Under 20%: a 1px danger hairline appears around the whole screen at 0.35. Under 10%: that hairline breathes at 0.80s. Nothing else in the addon ever changes colour.
A frame, not a vignette. The screen edge is one hairline inset 0 — no gradient, no bloom, no blur. It works because it's the only shape in the design that isn't a bar, and it's in your peripheral vision where a bar isn't.
Threshold marks in the trough. Two 1px ticks at the 20% and 40% positions: the 20% mark is black at 55% so it reads against the red fill, the 40% mark is white at 25% against the empty trough. You can see how close you are without reading the number.
Number size is the tell. Health goes 12pt → 14pt at 40%. It's the only type-size change in the whole spec, which is what makes it register.

Edit mode edits alpha, not just position. That's the structural difference from proposal 1: in a HUD where most things are invisible, "where is it" and "how loud is it" are the same question, so the inspector carries a rest-alpha slider next to the anchor and offset.
Every box prints its resting alpha. Ghost boxes are the group bbox + 4 units, hairline at 45% white, name in 10pt caps outside the box, resting value beside it. Reading the screen once tells you the whole configuration.
"Preview at rest" is the important button. It drops the dim and the boxes and applies the real alphas so you can judge the quiet state without leaving edit mode. Tab cycles frames, so a group at 0 alpha is still reachable by keyboard alone.
Reveal group is a property. Each frame declares which reveal channel owns it — always / cursor / combat / target / peek-only — which is how "hidden but findable" stops being a promise and becomes a setting.

STATE 1

At rest

Out of combat, no target, nothing pending. The entire addon is one 440-unit hairline at 8% on the left edge — the rail's baseline, present only so the rail is a place and not a surprise. Everything else sits at alpha 0 with its anchors intact.

Why leave the hairline. A rail that only exists when it has content teaches nothing. 1px at 8% is below notice while you play and unmistakable the moment you look for it — and it gives the dots a line to arrive on, so their entrance reads as "docking" rather than "appearing from nowhere".
What alpha 0 does not mean. Frames stay parented and anchored at all times — Rebar animates alpha, never Hide() — so a reveal is instant and a click-through never lands on an invisible-but-live frame. Mouse input is disabled below 0.05 alpha.
STATE 2

Notifications waiting · one hovered

Three things happened. Each docks on the rail as a coloured square that breathes between 100% and 55% alpha on a 2-second loop, with a soft bloom behind it at 35%. Hovering slides a label in from the rail and stops the breathing; clicking opens the real Blizzard frame and the dot leaves with a 1.4× scale-out. Ignoring is a first-class outcome: after 60 seconds a dot drops its ceiling to 40% and keeps waiting.
Quest ready — Where Dust Dances
CLICK OPENS THE QUEST LOG · 4m AGO
Colour is the only classifier. Five hues at matched lightness: whisper blue, quest gold, loot green, achievement violet, repair coral. No glyphs, no counts on the dot — the label carries the words, so the resting state stays wordless.
Stacking. Same type collapses: a second whisper thickens nothing, it adds "· 2" inside the label. Different types stack downward at 26-unit pitch, newest at the bottom, so the top dot is always the oldest grievance.
Hover, not click, to identify. Per your pick: hover explains, click hands off to the real frame. The dot never becomes a mini-inbox — Rebar is a positioning tool, not a chat client.
Bloom is one asset. A single 32 × 32 white radial PNG, tinted per type, drawn 6 units larger than the dot at 35% and scaling 1.0 ↔ 1.25 with the breath. It is the only texture Rebar ships.
STATE 3

In combat

Three stacked 240-unit bars under the character carry health, resource and cast; a 400-unit hairline at the top carries the target, with a tick per debuff I own whose height is its remaining duration. Above the stack, one tick per tracked cooldown: full height means ready. No icons, no panels, no numbers — the "76" only exists because health is below 100%.

Bar weights carry meaning. 4 units = something that kills you (health, cast). 2 units = something that only limits you (resource). Same length, same left edge, so all three read as one object at a glance.
Duration as height. Debuff ticks and cooldown ticks share one grammar: 12 units tall = full, shrinking downward. Two glances — top strip for the target, bottom strip for me.
Numbers are exceptions. Health prints a bare integer only under 100%, in white; under 40% it turns danger red and the bar breathes at 1.6s. Resource never prints. Cast prints seconds only.
Rail keeps working. Notifications don't hide in combat, they just stop breathing and sit at their 40% ceiling — you can still see one arrived without it competing with the fight.

This is the state that has to prove the layout still exists, so it shows every frame Rebar manages, in the same places it always keeps them.

MENU · LIT = MAIL
Action slots keep their footprint. 40 units with a 6-unit gutter and a 1px hairline; empty slots are hairline-only, filled slots carry the game's icon at 6% behind it in this mockup. Keybinds drop to 9pt and 45% white — legible on peek, invisible from memory.

Chat and tracker lose their panels entirely. Text on the world, no background, no border, 12pt with a 1px rule under the tracker header. If legibility over bright terrain fails, the fix is a 1-unit black text outline — not a panel.
Alt vs peek. Alt-held has no clock line and no timeout — it's for deliberate work. The peek key is for a glance, so it shows the drain so you know how long you've got before it leaves.
STATE 5

Cursor proximity — one group only

Moving the mouse into a frame group's zone fades that group alone to full in 0.15s; leaving starts a 0.4s grace, then a 0.35s fade out. Groups are independent, so the cursor never drags the whole HUD around with it. The magenta rectangle is documentation, not UI — it marks the 120-unit reveal zone around the action bar group.
